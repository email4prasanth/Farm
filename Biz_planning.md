# Small-Scale Business Planning – Key Considerations

## 1. Introduction

The following four business opportunities are being explored to understand their feasibility, investment requirements, production processes and marketing potential.

1. Dairy Farming
2. Paper Plate Manufacturing
3. Mushroom Cultivation
4. Cold-Pressed Oil Manufacturing

These businesses can be evaluated under two major operational segments:

- Production Management – Planning, infrastructure, procurement, production, quality and operational management.
- Marketing Management – Product positioning, pricing, distribution, sales and customer management.

## 2. Key Areas to Be Evaluated

The following factors need to be studied before starting any business.

1. Business Goal and Vision
2. Business Model
3. Land and Location
4. Selection of Appropriate Breed / Raw Materials / Components
5. Production Capacity and Planning
6. Feed and Raw Material Management
7. Health and Hygiene Management (where applicable)
8. Infrastructure and Equipment Requirements
9. Labour and Resource Management
10. Record Management (Income, Expenses and Profit & Loss)
11. Product Quality and Shelf Life
12. Product Packaging and Storage
13. Product Marketing and Distribution
14. Initial Investment and Working Capital

## 3. Prerequisites

The following qualities and preparations are essential before starting the business.

1. Patience: Understand that business growth and profitability take time.
2. Proper Management: Maintain systematic management of production, resources and operations.
3. Planning: Prepare a clear business plan with short-term and long-term objectives.
4. Market Research: Understand customer demand, competition, pricing and market expectations.
5. Appropriate Selection: Choose the right breed, raw materials, equipment or production components based on local climate and market requirements.
6. Resource Management: Ensure proper management of feed, raw materials, water, electricity and manpower.
7. Health and Hygiene Management: Maintain appropriate health, sanitation and preventive measures wherever applicable.
8. Marketing Strategy: Decide whether the business will focus on wholesale, retail, direct customers or a combination.
9. Financial Discipline: Monitor investment, operational expenses, cash flow and profitability.

## 4. Investment Requirements

Investment should be divided into initial capital expenditure and recurring operational expenses.

| Investment Category | Requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Land | Own land or leased land |
| Infrastructure | Construction of production and supporting facilities |
| Equipment | Machinery, tools and supporting equipment |
| Components | Livestock, seeds, raw materials or other production inputs |
| Labour | Workers, skilled labour and additional manpower |
| Utilities | Electricity, water and drainage |
| Consumables | Feed, packaging materials and other consumables |
| Healthcare | Medicines, vaccinations and veterinary expenses, where applicable |
| Transportation | Raw material procurement and product delivery |
| Working Capital | Money required to manage daily operations until revenue becomes stable |

## 5. Production Management

Production is the core operational activity. The following areas need to be evaluated.

### 5.1 Infrastructure and Location

1. Select an appropriate location with good road connectivity.
2. Ensure sufficient availability of electricity and water.
3. Provide proper drainage facilities.
4. Avoid flood-prone and waterlogging areas.
5. Plan infrastructure dimensions based on production capacity.
6. Divide the infrastructure into:
   - Core production area.
   - Supporting facilities.
   - Storage area.
   - Packaging and dispatch area.
7. Consider future expansion requirements during initial construction.

### 5.2 Raw Material and Resource Management

1. Ensure raw materials and feed are readily available.
2. Identify reliable suppliers.
3. Evaluate the cost and quality of raw materials.
4. Maintain sufficient stock to avoid production interruptions.
5. Where possible, allocate separate land for growing feed or other required raw materials.
6. Identify alternative suppliers in case of shortages.

### 5.3 Production Planning

1. Define the expected production capacity per day, week and month.
2. Understand the production cycle and time required for each batch.
3. Identify the expected output from each individual unit or batch.
4. Understand product shelf life and storage requirements.
5. Maintain hygiene and sanitation standards.
6. Establish quality control procedures.
7. Identify opportunities to minimise wastage.
8. Plan production according to market demand rather than producing excess stock.

### 5.4 Health and Hygiene Management

Particularly relevant to dairy farming and mushroom cultivation.

1. Understand possible diseases and health-related issues.
2. Identify preventive measures.
3. Maintain timely medical treatment and vaccination records, where applicable.
4. Understand methods to control external parasites.
5. Maintain regular cleaning and sanitation schedules.
6. Consult qualified professionals when necessary.

For manufacturing businesses, this section should focus on machinery maintenance, workplace safety, food safety and sanitation.

### 5.5 Record Management

Maintain records to understand operational performance and profitability.

| Record Type | Details to Maintain |
| --- | --- |
| Production Record | Output from each unit or batch |
| Raw Material Record | Quantity purchased, consumed and remaining |
| Feed Record | Feed quantity, consumption and expenses |
| Healthcare Record | Medicines, vaccinations and treatment |
| Labour Record | Employee attendance and salary |
| Maintenance Record | Equipment servicing and repair expenses |
| Inventory Record | Available stock and wastage |
| Customer Record | Customer details and delivery history |
| Sales Record | Daily, weekly and monthly sales |
| Financial Record | Income, expenses, cash flow and profit/loss |

### 5.6 Labour Management

1. Identify the number of workers required.
2. Understand which activities require skilled labour.
3. Estimate daily and monthly labour expenses.
4. Identify activities that can be automated.
5. Plan additional manpower during peak production periods.

## 6. Marketing Management

Production alone does not guarantee business success. A proper marketing and distribution strategy is equally important.

### 6.1 Sales and Distribution Channels

Identify the appropriate sales model.

1. Direct customer sales.
2. Home delivery.
3. Retail shops.
4. Wholesale distribution.
5. Supermarkets and departmental stores.
6. Restaurants, hotels and other bulk purchasers.
7. Online sales and social media platforms.

### 6.2 Pricing Strategy

1. Calculate the total production cost per unit.
2. Identify competitor pricing.
3. Determine wholesale and retail prices.
4. Define the expected profit margin.
5. Consider transportation and packaging expenses while fixing the selling price.

### 6.3 Payment Management

Define payment terms based on the customer category.

- Daily payments.
- Weekly payments.
- Monthly payments.
- Advance payments.
- Credit-based payments.

Maintain proper records of pending payments and outstanding balances.

### 6.4 Product Strategy

Evaluate how the product will be sold.

1. Fresh products.
2. Processed products.
3. Value-added products.
4. Bulk packaging.
5. Retail packaging.
6. Branded products.

### 6.5 Customer Management

1. Identify the target customer.
2. Understand customer expectations.
3. Maintain consistent product quality.
4. Ensure timely delivery.
5. Collect customer feedback.
6. Build long-term relationships with regular customers.

## 7. Additional Areas You Should Not Miss

These are particularly important because a business can have good production capacity but still fail due to poor financial planning or a lack of market demand.

| Area | What needs to be evaluated |
| --- | --- |
| Business Feasibility | Is there sufficient demand in the local market? |
| Competitor Analysis | Who are the existing competitors, and what are their prices? |
| Legal Requirements | What licences, registrations and approvals are required? |
| Financial Planning | What is the total investment and monthly operational cost? |
| Break-even Analysis | How many units must be sold to recover the investment and expenses? |
| Risk Management | What happens during disease outbreaks, raw material shortages or reduced demand? |
| Waste Management | How can production waste be reduced or converted into useful products? |
| Quality Certification | Are food safety or other product certifications required? |
| Scalability | Can the business expand without significantly increasing operational complexity? |
| Sustainability | Can the business operate profitably over the long term? |
