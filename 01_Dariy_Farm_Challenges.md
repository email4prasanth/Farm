# Dairy Farming – Key Challenges and Limitations

1. **Short Milk Shelf Life:** Raw milk has a limited shelf life (approximately 6–8 hours under ordinary conditions). Chilling, storage and processing require additional investment and effort.
2. **Continuous Monitoring:** Daily feeding, milking, cleaning and health monitoring limit personal time, family commitments and travel flexibility.
3. **Livestock Lifecycle:** Managing cattle after their productive life, including resale and ethical end-of-life considerations.
4. **High Space and Feed Requirements:** Requires sufficient land, regular fodder supply and significant maintenance expenses.
5. **Healthcare Management:** Timely vaccinations, veterinary care, breeding management and disease prevention add to operational costs.
6. **Manpower Dependency:** Difficult for a single family member to manage independently; additional workers are generally required.
7. **Limited Expansion Opportunities:** Expanding into curd, paneer, butter and ghee requires additional infrastructure, equipment, labour and marketing efforts, with strong market competition.
8. **Uncertain Market Demand:** Daily milk production must match customer demand to avoid wastage and financial losses.
9. **Fluctuating Operational Costs:** Feed prices, labour, veterinary expenses and transportation directly affect profitability.
10. **Waste Management:** Cattle dung, wastewater and farm sanitation require additional space and management.
11. **Milk Price Dependency:** Selling through collection centres or distributors may result in lower profit margins and limited pricing control.
12. **Productivity Fluctuations:** Milk yield varies depending on breed, age, health, climate and lactation cycle.

## Overall Assessment

Dairy farming requires high initial investment, continuous monitoring, manpower dependency and recurring operational expenses.
